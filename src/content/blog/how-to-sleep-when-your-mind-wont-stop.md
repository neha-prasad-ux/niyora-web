You are tired. Your body wants to sleep. But your mind is wide awake, running through tomorrow, replaying today, chasing worries in circles. The harder you try to sleep, the more awake you feel. Here is how to actually wind down.

The method has three steps: Reflect, Regulate, Respond. We call it R/R/R. Sleep is not something you do, it is something your body drops into once it feels safe enough. You cannot force it, so you settle the body and get out of its way.

## Reflect: name that you are wired, not sleepy

The first step is to notice the state you are in and name it. "My body is tired but my system is still switched on." "I am wired, not sleepy." "My mind is looping, not solving."

This matters because fighting to sleep keeps you awake. The frustration of "why am I still up" is its own kind of alarm. Naming the state as your system running late, not a problem to solve right now, takes the pressure off, and the pressure is half of what is keeping you up.

If you are in the days before your period, this gets stickier. Your brain drifts toward the heavy, worst-case version of any thought this week, and it does it most at night. Knowing that is the pre-period tilt, not the truth, makes it easier to stop believing the 2am version of everything.

## Regulate: bring your body down for rest

You will not think your way to sleep. Settle the body and rest comes to meet you.

- **Long, slow out-breaths.** Breathe in for four, out for six or seven, and keep going for a few minutes. The slow out-breath is the switch that moves your body from alert toward rest. This one does more than the rest.
- **Relax downward.** Starting at your forehead, slowly soften each part of your body down to your feet. Unclench your jaw. Let your shoulders drop. Let your hands go heavy.
- **Cool and dark.** Lower the temperature if you can, and keep screens out of reach. Heat and bright light both tell your body to stay awake.
- **Feel the weight.** Notice the weight of your body sinking into the bed and the blanket over you. This pulls attention out of your head and into the room.

You do not have to feel sleepy to start. You settle, and the sleepiness follows.

## Respond: set the thoughts down for now

If a thought keeps circling, park it. Keep a notepad by the bed and write the worry in one line. "Handle the email in the morning." Writing it down tells your brain it is safe to let go, because the thought will not be lost.

You are not ignoring the problem. You are picking a better time to face it, when your head is clear and the thought is smaller. Most things that feel enormous at midnight are ordinary by morning. If sleep still will not come, that is okay. Rest with your eyes closed and your breathing slow. Quiet rest counts, and it often tips into sleep once you stop chasing it.

## The short version

Name that you are wired, not sleepy. Slow your out-breath and relax your body downward. Write the worry down and set it aside until morning.
