A panic spiral has a shape. One anxious thought sparks a physical reaction, your racing heart makes you more scared, the fear feeds the next thought, and it loops tighter and faster. It can feel like something is seriously wrong. Here is what to do while it is happening.

The method has three steps: Reflect, Regulate, Respond. We call it R/R/R. Panic is your body's alarm going off with no real fire. You cannot argue an alarm into silence, so you calm the body first, then steady the mind.

## Reflect: name it as panic

The moment you notice the spiral, name it. "This is a panic spiral." "This is my alarm going off, not a real emergency."

This one line matters because panic lies about what is happening. It tells you that you are in danger, that this will not stop, that something is truly wrong. Naming it as panic reminds you it is a wave. It rises, it peaks, and it always comes back down. You have come down from every single one before this.

If you are in the days before your period, panic can arrive more easily and feel more intense. Your built-in brake on fear eases off this week, so your alarm is set extra sensitive. Knowing that helps: this is your system turned up, not a sign that anything is actually wrong.

## Regulate: calm your body fast

This is the most important step, and it works. Bring the physical alarm down and the fear has nothing to feed on.

- **Slow the out-breath.** Breathe in for four, out for six or more. Panic makes you breathe fast and shallow, which keeps the alarm ringing. A long, slow out-breath is the fastest way to switch it off. Keep going even if it feels hard at first.
- **Ground through your senses.** Name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch, two you can smell, one you can taste. This drags your attention out of the spiral and anchors it to the real, safe room around you.
- **Cold on your skin.** Cold water on your face or an ice cube in your hand. The shock of cold interrupts the panic and slows your heart.
- **Feet on the floor.** Press your feet down and feel the ground hold you. Say where you are and that you are safe.

Do not try to make the panic vanish. Just help the wave crest. It will pass faster than the fear tells you.

## Respond: steady yourself as it fades

As the peak drops, be gentle with what comes next. Do not jump straight back into whatever set it off. Give yourself a few minutes.

Take slow sips of water. Keep breathing steady. Remind yourself of the plain fact: that was panic, it passed, and you handled it. If a specific worry triggered it, write it in one line to look at later, when you are steady and it will feel smaller.

If panic spirals are happening often, or they scare you, it is worth talking to a doctor. Learning to ride out one spiral and knowing when to get support are not opposites. They work together.

## The short version

Name it as panic. Slow your out-breath and ground through your senses until the wave passes. Then steady yourself gently before going back to your day.
