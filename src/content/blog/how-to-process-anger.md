Anger usually gives you two bad options in the moment. Explode, and hurt someone or wreck the thing you care about. Or bottle it, swallow it down, and carry it around until it leaks out sideways later. There is a third way, and it starts with treating anger as information, not an emergency.

The method has three steps: Reflect, Regulate, Respond. We call it R/R/R. Anger is a fast, physical surge, so you cannot reason with it while it is peaking. You cool the body first, then decide what to actually do.

## Reflect: name the anger and what is under it

First, name it plainly. "I am angry." Not "I am fine." Not the sharp thing you want to say. Just the fact of it.

Then look one layer down. Anger is often a bodyguard for something softer underneath. Ask yourself what is really there. Hurt? Feeling dismissed? Exhausted and stretched too thin? Scared? The anger is real, and it is usually pointing at a real need that got stepped on.

Before your period, the calm that normally absorbs small frustrations runs thin. So things you would brush off any other day feel genuinely unbearable, and the anger is turned all the way up. That does not make it fake. It means the volume knob is high this week, which is exactly why the next step matters more than usual.

## Regulate: cool the surge before you act

Anger peaks fast and then falls. Your job is to not act while it is peaking. Buy your body a little time.

- **Step away.** Leave the room if you can, even for two minutes. Distance is not avoiding the issue. It is refusing to handle it at full boil.
- **Long out-breaths.** In for four, out for six. Do it until the heat in your chest drops a notch.
- **Move it through.** Anger is energy in the body. Walk hard, push against a wall, shake out your hands. Give the surge somewhere to go that is not a person.
- **Cold water.** On your face or wrists. It brings the physical spike down quickly.

You are not swallowing the anger. You are letting the wave crest and pass so you can think.

## Respond: say the real thing, on purpose

Once the heat drops, you get to choose your response instead of firing off a reaction. Now the anger becomes useful.

Name the need under it, calmly and directly. "I felt dismissed when you decided that without me." "I am stretched too thin and I need help with this." That is the difference between exploding and expressing. One dumps the feeling on someone. The other tells them what you actually need.

And sometimes the honest response is later. "I am too heated to talk about this well right now. Can we come back to it tonight?" That is not bottling it. That is choosing a better moment on purpose.

## The short version

Name the anger and what is under it. Cool the surge before you act. Then say the real thing on purpose, or pick a better time to say it.
