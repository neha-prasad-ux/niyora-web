You feel it before it happens. Something small lands wrong, a tone, a dish left out, a question at the wrong moment, and you can feel the sharp reply loading. The person in front of you is the person you love, and they are about to catch something that is not really about them.

Here is how to catch it before it lands. The method has three steps: Reflect, Regulate, Respond. We call it R/R/R. The urge to snap starts in a body running hot, so you cannot just decide to be nicer. You settle the body first, and the sharp edge softens.

## Reflect: catch the charge before it fires

The whole game is the gap between feeling it and saying it. Name what is happening in that gap. "I am about to snap." "My alarm is running hot and this person is just the closest." "This is bigger than what they actually did."

That last one is the key. The frustration is real, but this week it is turned all the way up, so a small thing reads as a large one. Naming it as your system running hot, not the other person being the problem, buys you the two seconds you need.

If you are in the days before your period, this is sharper. Your built-in brake on frustration eases off this week, so things you would shrug off any other day land like an insult. The people closest to you catch it because they are closest, not because they earned it.

## Regulate: settle before you speak

You cannot think your way calm mid-surge. Give your body a minute first.

- **Step away.** Say "give me a minute" and leave the room. This is not avoiding the moment. It is refusing to have it while your alarm is screaming.
- **Cold water.** Splash cold water on your face. It resets your system in seconds and takes the heat out of the impulse.
- **Slow the out-breath.** Breathe in for four, out for six. A few of these and the urge to snap loses its fuel.
- **Feet and jaw.** Press your feet to the floor and unclench your jaw. The body holding tension is the body ready to fire.

You do not have to feel warm and calm to come back. You just have to be a notch below the boil.

## Respond: say the real thing

Once your body is quieter, your head can work again. Now you can say what you actually mean instead of the sharp version. "I am on edge right now and it is not about you." "Can we come back to this later." Naming your state is not an excuse. It is honesty, and it protects the person in front of you.

If you did snap before you caught it, repair. A short, honest message later shows one hard moment is not the whole story. "I was short with you earlier and I am sorry. Rough day in my body." Repair is not weakness. It is what keeps love steady through a hard hour.

None of this means swallowing your feelings. It means giving your body a minute to catch up with you before you speak, so the words land where you mean them to.

## The short version

Catch the charge and name it as your system running hot. Step away, cool down, slow your breath. Then say the real thing, and repair if you missed the gap.
