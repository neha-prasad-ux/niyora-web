Sometimes it hits all at once. Your chest goes tight, your thoughts speed up, and every small thing feels like one thing too many. You need to calm down, and you need it to work now, not in an hour.

Here is a method that actually fits how this works. It has three steps: Reflect, Regulate, Respond. We call it R/R/R. You cannot think your way out of a feeling that started in the body, so this settles the body first, then clears the head.

## Reflect: name what is happening

The fastest first move is the smallest one. Name the feeling out loud or in your head. "I am overwhelmed." "I am anxious." "My hands feel shaky."

Naming a feeling takes some of its charge away. It also puts a small gap between you and the feeling, so you are looking at it instead of drowning in it. You do not need the perfect word. "Too much" is a fine start.

If you are in the days before your period, this can arrive faster and hit harder than usual. That is not you being dramatic. Your nervous system is more sensitive this week, so a normal load reads as a bigger one. Naming it as "this is the pre-period version of overwhelmed" can take some of the fear out of it.

## Regulate: settle your body in about a minute

Now bring the alarm down. Pick one of these and do it for sixty seconds.

- **Paced breathing.** Breathe in for four counts, out for six. The long out-breath is the part that tells your body the danger has passed. Do this for a minute and your heart rate follows.
- **Cold water.** Splash cold water on your face, or hold something cold against your cheeks. This triggers a reflex that slows your system down in seconds.
- **Ground through your senses.** Name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch. This pulls your attention out of the spiral and back into the room.
- **Feet and floor.** Press both feet flat on the ground and feel the contact. Unclench your jaw and drop your shoulders.

You do not have to feel calm to start. You start, and the calm follows.

## Respond: do the next small thing

Once your body is a notch quieter, your head can work again. Do not try to fix everything. Pick one small thing and finish it. Reply to one message. Put one cup in the sink. Write the worry down to deal with later.

Control does not come from doing everything. It comes from doing one thing. That single finished task is proof you can still move, and it breaks the "everything is too much" feeling into something you can hold.

## The short version

Name it. Settle your body for a minute. Do one small thing. That is the whole loop, and you can run it anywhere.
