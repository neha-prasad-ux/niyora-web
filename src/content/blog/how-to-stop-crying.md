The tears come and they will not stop. Maybe something set them off, maybe nothing did. You feel silly, or embarrassed, or scared that you cannot pull yourself together. You want it to stop, and you want that now.

Here is a method that fits how crying actually works. It has three steps: Reflect, Regulate, Respond. We call it R/R/R. Crying is a physical release, not a decision, so you cannot order it to stop. You settle the body first, and the tears ease on their own.

## Reflect: name what is happening

The first move is small. Name it. "I am crying and I cannot stop." "I feel heavy and it is spilling over." You do not need a reason. Sometimes there is no single cause, just a lot of pressure that found a way out.

Naming it takes some of the panic out of the crying itself. A lot of what makes it worse is the second wave, the fear that you are falling apart. You are not. This is a release, and releases end.

If you are in the days before your period, this arrives faster and runs deeper. The part of you that reaches for steadiness is turned down this week, so a normal amount of feeling spills over more easily. Knowing that is the pre-period version of this, not a sign anything is wrong, can take the fear out of the tears.

## Regulate: settle your body

You cannot stop crying by force, but you can bring your body down a notch, and the tears follow. Pick one and do it for a minute.

- **Slow the out-breath.** Breathe in for four, out for six or more. Crying comes with fast, catching breaths. A long, steady out-breath tells your body the wave is passing. It also smooths the ragged breathing that keeps the crying going.
- **Cold water.** Splash cold water on your face or hold something cold against your cheeks. The shock of cold interrupts the surge and slows your system in seconds.
- **Feet and ground.** Press both feet flat on the floor and feel it hold you. Say where you are out loud. This pulls you out of the feeling and back into the room.
- **Let it finish.** If the tears want to come, let them for a minute or two. A good cry that runs its course often clears faster than one you fight.

You do not have to feel calm to start. You start, and the calm follows.

## Respond: be gentle as it fades

As the crying eases, go slow. Take sips of water. Splash your face, breathe, and give yourself a few minutes before you jump back into anything.

Do not add a layer of judgment on top. You cried. It passed. That is a body doing what bodies do. If a specific worry set it off, write it in one line to look at later, when you are steadier and it will feel smaller.

If you are crying most days, or it comes with a heaviness that will not lift, it is worth talking to a doctor. Riding out one hard moment and getting real support are not opposites. They work together.

## The short version

Name it without needing a reason. Slow your out-breath and let the wave finish. Then be gentle with yourself before going back to your day.
