The lights are off, the day is done, and your brain picks that exact moment to replay every awkward thing you said and every problem you have not solved. The more you try to stop, the louder it gets. Here is how to actually break the loop.

The method has three steps: Reflect, Regulate, Respond. We call it R/R/R. Overthinking at night is a body problem wearing a thinking costume. Your system is still switched on when it should be winding down, so we settle the body first, then quiet the mind.

## Reflect: notice you are looping

The first step is to catch it and name it. "I am overthinking." "I am running the same worry on a loop." "This is my brain replaying, not solving."

That last one matters. At night, the mind rarely produces new answers. It just circles the same worries and makes them feel bigger. Naming it as a loop, not a to-do list, gives you permission to step out of it.

Before your period this gets stickier. Your brain is more likely to drift toward the heavy, self-critical, worst-case version of any thought this week, and it does it at night especially. Knowing that is the pre-period tilt talking, not the truth, makes it easier to not believe every 2am thought.

## Regulate: bring your body down for sleep

You will not think your way calm. Settle the body and the mind quiets on its own.

- **Long out-breaths.** Breathe in for four, out for six or seven. Keep going for a minute or two. The slow out-breath is the switch that moves your body toward rest.
- **Body scan downward.** Starting at your head, slowly relax each part down to your feet. Unclench your jaw. Let your shoulders drop away from your ears.
- **Cool and dark.** Lower the temperature if you can, and keep screens away. Bright light and heat both tell your body to stay awake.
- **Feel the weight.** Notice the weight of your body on the bed and the blanket over you. This pulls attention out of your head and into the room.

## Respond: set the thoughts down for now

If a thought keeps circling, park it. Keep a notepad by the bed and write the worry in one line. "Deal with the email in the morning." Writing it down tells your brain it is safe to stop holding the thought, because it will not be lost.

You are not ignoring the problem. You are picking a better time to face it, when your head is clearer and the thought is smaller. Most things that feel enormous at midnight are ordinary by morning.

## The short version

Name the loop. Slow your breathing and relax your body downward. Write the worry down and set it aside until morning.
