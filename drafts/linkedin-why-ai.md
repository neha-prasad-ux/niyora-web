Why I embrace AI a little tighter than most.

Some honest thoughts.

I moved from being a doctor to being a designer. That meant working in a hospital through Covid by day, and learning design at night. It came with a lot of self doubt, a lot of tears, and a lot of falling back.

Guess what happened.

I got a job. I work in a sector I love. I enjoy what I do so much that I look forward to tomorrow.

That experience got me used to constant upskilling. It started as a way out. My parents don't have a boat or a cabin to fall back on. There is no safety net if I am bad at my job. The real engine, I think, was the fear of becoming irrelevant.

But somewhere in the last few months, it changed. I actually enjoy it now.

I took a story writing course.
I started reading books again.
I paint, once in a while.
I studied UX motion design in depth and wrote about it from the basics of physics.
I explored soft skills and built a quiz for designers to reflect on themselves.
AI is just the newest thing on that list.

Back in college, I wanted to research the impact of deep breathing on stress. I couldn't take it far. It got complex fast, and I let it go.

Now I am building Niyora, in the open, so I can finally answer that old question for myself.

I am a mortal being. I want to enjoy my life doing what I love and sharing it with others. AI has given me wings, and I feel good about that.

So if you want to know why I use AI:
it took me from the fear of being irrelevant to enjoying what I learn every day.
It ticks every box I have.
