A man is walking through the forest when he sees a tiger.

He runs. Faster than he ever has.

His brain says: you just ate, I need to start digesting.
He says: not now.
Brain says: no problem.

His brain says: you need to breathe out and slow down.
He says: not now.
Brain says: no problem.

Pee? A bowel movement?
Not now.
No problem.

Then, with no warning, his legs give out. He is completely spent.

He asks his brain: why didn't you warn me?
The brain says: I switched everything off so you could survive. There was no warning left to give.

He turns around. The tiger is nowhere to be seen. He is in the middle of the city.

He didn't outrun the tiger. He ran long after it was gone, and only stopped when his body made the choice for him.

By then it was too late.

That is pretty much what happens when we are stressed in front of a computer. There is no tiger. There never was. But the body runs anyway, day after day, and because there is no finish line and no way to feel how tired we are getting, it never gets the signal to stand down.

The good news is that the signal is simple to send.

Slow breathing and cool air are two of the easiest ways to do it. A long exhale, or cold air on your face, gently flips the body out of fight-or-flight and back into rest, through the vagus nerve. It tells the brain the chase is over. You just have to do it on purpose, before the body has to do it for you.
